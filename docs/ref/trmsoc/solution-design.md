# TRMSOC-1:2024 — Solution Design

Reference document: **TRMSOC-1:2024 — Technical Requirements for Managed SOC Service Providers**, issued by the National Cybersecurity Authority (NCA) of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.
Companion document: **RFMSOC-1:2024 — Regulatory Framework for Licensing Managed Security Operations Center (MSOC) Services**.

This document maps the 39 technical requirements in TRMSOC-1:2024 to the engineering design of this MDR platform (a Wazuh-based fork). It is the working reference for build, audit, and qualification. It is derived directly from the regulator-published PDFs in this repository (`f.txt.pdf` is TRMSOC, `rfmsoc-AR.pdf` is RFMSOC).

### 1.1 Two-tier service model

TRMSOC §5 defines a two-tier licensing model for MSOC service providers. Each technical requirement carries a Tier 1 and Tier 2 obligation, drawn from the values: **Required**, **Required (during business hours, minimum)**, **Recommended**, or **Not Required**. The three pillars considered across all requirements are **Operations**, **Technologies**, and **People**.

The mapping below records the obligation per tier where it is unambiguous from the source. Where the source indicates a partial obligation (e.g. "Required partially during business hours minimum"), that wording is preserved.

## 2. Definitions used by TRMSOC

The following terms are used throughout this design document with the meanings given in TRMSOC §2:

- **Authority** — the National Cybersecurity Authority (NCA).
- **Entities** — government, private and other entities consuming MSOC services.
- **Framework** — the Regulatory Framework for Licensing MSOC Services (RFMSOC).
- **Policy** — the National Policy for Security Operations Centers.
- **Critical National Infrastructure (CNI)** — infrastructure whose disruption has a significant impact on availability of essential services or on national security and defense.
- **SOC** — Security Operations Center.
- **Managed SOC services** — the SOC services consumed by service beneficiaries from a licensed provider.
- **License** — the document issued by NCA permitting service provision.
- **Service provider** — the entity licensed by NCA to deliver MSOC services.
- **Managed SOC** — the SOC operated by the service provider that delivers the licensed service.
- **Service beneficiary** — the entity that contracts the service provider for MSOC services.
- **Qualification certificate** — the certificate issued by NCA to natural persons authorized to work as MSOC analysts.
- **Managed SOC analyst** — a natural person who holds a valid qualification certificate.
- **Cloud service provider** — natural or legal person providing cloud computing services.
- **Cybersecurity event** / **incident** — events that affect (event) or compromise (incident) the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of an entity's assets.
- **Cybersecurity controls issued by the Authority** — minimum-baseline rules issued by NCA.
- **Service provider's cybersecurity program** — the documents, policies, processes and resources implementing the provider's own cybersecurity posture.
- **Tier 1 / Tier 2** — the two MSOC service licensing tiers.
- **SOC infrastructure** — the hardware, software, networking, and supporting facilities that run the SOC.
- **IT / OT** — information technology / operational technology.
- **Event log** — a record produced by IT/OT systems containing cybersecurity-relevant data.
- **Cybersecurity telemetry** — data generated by IT/OT systems used for monitoring and detection.
- **Cybersecurity incident** — an event that affects confidentiality, integrity, or availability and is classified by the SOC as an incident.
- **Haseen portal** — the National Portal for Cybersecurity Services operated by NCA.

## 3. Objective and scope of this design

Per TRMSOC §3 and §4, this design demonstrates how the platform satisfies the technical requirements applicable to the Tier the operator is licensed for. It is the source of truth for:

- the SOC service catalogue and SLAs (Req. 2)
- the data model and parsing layer for telemetry ingestion (Req. 22, 25–28, 30)
- the detection and hunting stack (Req. 9, 10, 31–35)
- the response and incident-management workflow (Req. 11–13)
- the logging, retention, and disposal lifecycle (Req. 17, 22, 24)
- the integration interfaces with NCA (Haseen) and external intelligence sources (Req. 8, 20, 36, 39)
- the operational, capacity, and isolation guarantees (Req. 4, 17, 21, 38)

## 4. Technical requirements — TRMSOC §5

The 39 numbered requirements below are the verbatim subject matter of TRMSOC §5. The "Solution" column states how this MDR platform satisfies each.

### Req. 1 — End-to-end SOC capability sequence

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC service provider to operate end-to-end SOC service touchpoints and elements considering the logical sequence of cybersecurity telemetry and service progress stages from monitoring and detection to analysis and investigation, without any failure or gaps in the sequence of processes and procedures, which enable efficient value delivery.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** The platform pipelines telemetry through a fixed sequence: ingest → parse/normalize → enrich → correlate → detect → triage → analyze → investigate → respond. Each stage is owned by a named module (see `src/ingestion`, `src/detection`, `src/case-mgmt`, `src/response`) with documented hand-offs.

### Req. 2 — SOC service catalogue

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to develop a SOC Service Catalogue which includes at minimum, the following elements for each sub-service offered: description, service level agreement (SLA), coverage, onboarding and off-boarding procedures, prerequisites, workflows, hours of operation, activities, RACI matrix and key interactions.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** The catalogue lives at `docs/service-catalogue/` with one document per sub-service; each follows a fixed template covering all ten elements above. SLA targets are defined in `docs/service-catalogue/sla.md`.

### Req. 3 — Process interoperability

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC service provider to demonstrate process interoperability — processes and procedures integrated at certain points and elements considering the logical sequence of cybersecurity telemetry and service progress stages.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Inter-stage contracts are formalised as event schemas in `schemas/`; downstream stages refuse malformed events and surface schema violations as platform alerts.

### Req. 4 — 24/7/365 operation

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC service provider to demonstrate ability to operate the services 24/7/365 in accordance with SLA and contractual obligations.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required (partially — at least during business hours). Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Shift coverage is defined in `docs/operations/shifts.md`. The platform's control plane is deployed active-active across two regions. Out-of-hours coverage for Tier 1 customers is documented in their SLA addendum.

### Req. 5 — Vendor support contracts for core technology

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC service provider to demonstrate support and maintenance contractual agreements with vendors for core technology platforms.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Vendor list and contract numbers tracked in `docs/operations/vendor-contracts.md` (controlled access). Support SLAs from upstream vendors must be at least equal to commitments to service beneficiaries.

### Req. 6 — Licensed CSP for cloud-delivered capabilities

**Requirement.** For cloud-delivered capabilities, the Managed SOC Service Provider to ensure subscription with a licensed cloud service provider (CSP) for a specific class. (See CST CCRF: https://www.cst.gov.sa/en/RulesandSystems/RegulatoryDocuments/Pages/CCRF.aspx)

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** All cloud-delivered components run on CSPs that hold a class license under CCRF. CSP class evidence is recorded in `docs/operations/csp-licensing.md`.

### Req. 7 — Compliance with NCA Cloud Cybersecurity Controls

**Requirement.** For cloud-delivered capabilities, the Managed SOC service provider to comply with NCA Cloud Cybersecurity Controls (CCC-1:2020).

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** A control-by-control mapping for CCC-1:2020 lives at `docs/compliance/ccc-mapping.md`. CCC compliance is asserted as part of release gating.

### Req. 8 — Information sharing with NCA via Haseen

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC service provider to fully comply with regulatory requirements issued by NCA related to cybersecurity information sharing, including but not limited to:
- Reporting cybersecurity alerts in accordance with NCA's instructions to ensure that notifications are sent to Service Beneficiaries and NCA through Haseen portal.
- Ingest/act upon information provided by NCA or Service Beneficiary. Information received from NCA through Haseen must be acted upon within the period determined by NCA.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Bidirectional Haseen integration in `src/integrations/haseen/`. Outbound alert and incident reports follow NCA's required schema; inbound NCA advisories are converted into platform tickets with NCA-set due dates.

### Req. 9 — Use case framework with MITRE ATT&CK

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC service provider to establish a use case framework with clear detection logic, mapped to leading platforms including MITRE ATT&CK.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Each detection rule carries one or more `attack.technique` tags. The use-case index at `detections/index.yaml` cross-references ATT&CK tactic/technique IDs.

### Req. 10 — Continuous use-case and correlation tuning

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC service provider to establish a methodology to continuously create and tune use-cases and correlation rules to augment detection and reduce false-positive alerts.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** A weekly tuning cadence is documented in `docs/detection-engineering/tuning-cadence.md`. False-positive rate per rule is tracked; rules above thresholds are routed for review.

### Req. 11 — Incident management compliance and Haseen reporting

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC service provider to fully comply with regulatory requirements issued by NCA related to cybersecurity incident management, including but not limited to: immediately notifying NCA and Service beneficiaries of cybersecurity incidents and their related information upon classifying security alert(s) as an incident. NCA notification shall be through the Haseen portal.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Classifying a case as an "incident" in the platform automatically (a) notifies the affected service beneficiary through their nominated channels and (b) files a Haseen incident report through the integration in `src/integrations/haseen/`.

### Req. 12 — Pre-defined response playbooks

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to establish pre-defined response playbooks for threats.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Playbooks live in `playbooks/` keyed by threat family (ransomware, BEC, credential theft, lateral movement, data exfiltration, DDoS, supply-chain). Each playbook is a versioned YAML file consumed by the response engine.

### Req. 13 — Containment methodology

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to define a methodology to contain cyber incidents as soon as they occur, which must include inventorying the affected assets, determining their classification, and providing initial recommendations regarding the actions required to be taken by the service beneficiaries and/or the service provider — when requested by NCA — to contain the cyber incident, stopping and reducing their damage, designing countermeasures, determining the estimated resources required and the time required for implementation, and mechanisms for following up on the implementation of recommendations.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** The containment workflow in `docs/response/containment.md` produces a structured containment package per incident: affected-asset inventory, asset classification, initial recommendations, time/resource estimates, follow-up checkpoints. The package is appended to the case record.

### Req. 14 — Orchestration and automation

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to demonstrate technical capability to connect, exchange, interpret and access data of different systems and solutions using orchestration and automation technologies.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** A SOAR layer (`src/soar/`) drives orchestration with a connector plug-in model. Built-in connectors cover EDR, NDR, IAM, ITSM, email security, and the Haseen portal.

### Req. 15 — Service portal with dashboards

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to build and avail a service portal that encompasses dashboards and reports for service beneficiaries providing visibility on service status and key metrics, including service-specific templates. Key types of dashboards include, but not limited to:
- Service status dashboards
- Executive-level dashboards highlighting key metrics and SLA compliance posture
- Operational dashboards providing visibility on operational metrics

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Recommended.

**Solution.** The beneficiary portal (`src/portal/`) ships three dashboard templates: status, executive, operational. Each can be branded per beneficiary.

### Req. 16 — Live data visualization

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to provide live data visualization including, but not limited to, detailed operational and service level graphs and charts to clients.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Recommended.

**Solution.** The portal streams live metrics over WebSockets; backing graphs are rendered from real-time platform telemetry rather than periodic snapshots.

### Req. 17 — Logical isolation of beneficiary data

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to establish logical isolation capabilities for service beneficiaries' data.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Per-beneficiary isolation is enforced at storage (per-tenant indices and KMS-managed keys), at query time (mandatory tenant predicate), and at the application layer (tenant-scoped IAM). Telemetry must additionally be securely disposed of in accordance with the NCA Data Cybersecurity Controls (DCC).

### Req. 18 — Tech-stack secure integration with auth providers

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to demonstrate technology stack secure integration capability with authentication and authorization services (for example LDAP, OAuth, Radius).

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Identity integrations live in `src/auth/` with adapters for LDAP, OAuth 2.0/OIDC, SAML, and RADIUS. MFA is enforced at IdP for analyst access.

### Req. 19 — Near-real-time intel sharing via STIX/TAXII and APIs

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to demonstrate near-real time external information gathering and sharing capability through automated integration of a dedicated platform utilizing STIX/TAXII standards and the utilization of API endpoint to send or receive relevant information including, but not limited to, cybersecurity telemetry, incidents, threat indicators and TTPs.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** The TIP component speaks STIX 2.1 over TAXII 2.1, and exposes a REST API for non-STIX peers. Indicators are pushed to the detection engine within minutes of receipt.

### Req. 20 — Dedicated SOC and supporting facilities

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to build and demonstrate a dedicated designated SOC and supporting physical facilities, networks and infrastructure for managed service delivery, adaptive to service delivery, security, scalability and other regulatory requirements.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Physical SOC location and access controls documented in `docs/operations/facilities.md`. The SOC network is segmented from corporate IT; production telemetry is processed on infrastructure dedicated to MSOC service delivery.

### Req. 21 — Telemetry collection per Annex B and Annex C

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to ensure the collection, monitoring and analysis of logs from log sources in accordance to Appendix B and Appendix C including but not limited to endpoints, applications, network devices, and security devices.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Annex B's event-log attributes are enforced at the parser layer (`parsers/`). Annex C's minimum telemetry classes are mandated by the onboarding checklist; missing classes block onboarding.

### Req. 22 — Centralized collection, aggregation, normalization

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to provide centralized collection, aggregation, and normalization of collected cybersecurity telemetry.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Wazuh's indexer + manager handles centralized collection. Aggregation and normalization happen in the parsing pipeline; the canonical event schema is in `schemas/event.json`.

### Req. 23 — 18-month retention with 12 months active

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to ensure that logs are stored for not less than 18 months. While logs that are related to that the last 12 months are actively available — Active Storage — to enable direct search and threat hunting activities.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Storage tiers: hot/warm (0–12 months, fully searchable) and cold (12–18 months, restorable). Retention enforcement runs nightly and is logged. Beneficiary contracts inherit these defaults; longer retention is opt-in.

### Req. 24 — Network flow collection

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to ensure the collection, monitoring and analysis of network flows including but not limited to NetFlow, JFlow, IPFIX, and sFlow information.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Recommended.

**Solution.** A flow collector (`src/ingestion/flows/`) accepts NetFlow v5/v9, IPFIX, JFlow and sFlow. Flow records are normalized into the same event schema and used for east–west visibility.

### Req. 25 — Critical packet capture per Annex C

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to ensure the collection, monitoring and analysis of critical network packets as per Appendix C while collection and storage of raw data must be at the entity side without transferring externally. In addition, the analysis and storage is to be performed after SSL decryption/offloading where possible.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Recommended.

**Solution.** A beneficiary-side packet-capture node (deployed on the entity's premises) holds raw PCAP for the 72-hour window prescribed by Annex C. Decrypted plaintext analysis is performed where the entity provides SSL offload.

### Req. 26 — Custom connectors for log formats

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to develop custom cybersecurity telemetry connectors and parsers to support the different security event log format interpretation including but not limited to, XML, CSV, JSON, CEF, and LEEF.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** The parser SDK in `parsers/sdk/` standardizes new connectors. Parsers exist out-of-box for XML, CSV, JSON, CEF, LEEF, key-value pairs, and syslog.

### Req. 27 — Multiple secure data-collection mechanisms

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to support multiple secure data collection mechanisms such as protocols including but not limited to syslog, WMI, SFTP, SCP, and HTTPS.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Ingestion endpoints expose syslog (TLS), WMI/WinRM, SFTP, SCP, and HTTPS APIs. Plain-text protocols are disabled by default.

### Req. 28 — Multi-stage correlation

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to demonstrate capability for multi-stage correlation.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** The detection engine supports stateful, multi-stage rules that combine signals across time, asset, identity, and source.

### Req. 29 — Custom telemetry parsers per source

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to demonstrate capability for the development of custom telemetry connectors and parsers to connect to and interpret different telemetry sources.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Same SDK as Req. 26; per-source parsers are versioned in `parsers/<source>/` with unit tests covering the canonical schema mapping.

### Req. 30 — Automated deduplication

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to demonstrate capability for automated deduplication of telemetry sources.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** The pipeline computes a canonical event hash and drops duplicates within a sliding window per tenant. Deduplication metrics surface on the operational dashboard (Req. 15).

### Req. 31 — Search, filter and threat hunting

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to support searching, filtering and hunting activities in near real-time and historical cybersecurity telemetry using appropriate tools and expressions to achieve the following:
- Develop and validate hypothesis through analysis of networks, endpoints and datasets to hunt threats that may have evaded existing use-cases and controls
- Hunt for indicators including hash values, IP addresses, and domain names
- Hunt for host and network artifacts, tools and attacker TTPs

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** The hunting workbench provides a query DSL, saved hunts, and a hypothesis library tied to ATT&CK techniques. Indicator-based and behavioural hunts are first-class.

### Req. 32 — Threat detection (signature, behaviour, anomaly, retro-matching)

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to implement threat detection for historical and near real-time threats based on the following:
- Signature-based and behavior-based detection to uncover anomalous and/or malicious activities
- Detect anomalies and outliers using statistical methods against established baselines
- Threat intelligence feeds to provide retro-matching capability

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** The detection engine combines signatures, behavioural rules, statistical anomaly detection, and TI retro-matching against the warm tier for the past 12 months (per Req. 23).

### Req. 33 — Data science / ML for advanced detection

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to demonstrate capability to apply data science and machine learning methods, including custom models, for advanced threat detection and event classification.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Recommended. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** The ML platform (`src/ml/`) hosts beneficiary-scoped models. Currently shipped: rare-process detection, login-anomaly scoring, DGA classification.

### Req. 34 — Custom detection rules and use cases

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to demonstrate capability for creating and implementing custom detection rules and use cases and continuously update them.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Detection content is code; rules live in `detections/` with CI checks (lint, unit test, replay against historical telemetry) before merge.

### Req. 35 — Native ingestion of community rule formats (SIGMA, YARA)

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to support automated ingestion of common rule formats according to best practices including but not limited to SIGMA and YARA, and natively executing rules.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** The detection engine compiles SIGMA to its native rule language at load time; YARA executes against file-event payloads via the EDR connector.

### Req. 36 — Continuous threat intelligence gathering

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to ensure continuous automated gathering of intelligence feeds from commercial sources, open and public sources, from specific industries and sectors, and the dark web; and to fully comply with regulatory requirements issued by NCA related to cybersecurity information sharing.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** TI feeds are ingested by the TIP (Req. 19). Dark-web monitoring uses sector-specific keyword sets approved per beneficiary. Sharing back to NCA goes through Haseen (Req. 8).

### Req. 37 — Sandbox malware analysis

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to demonstrate capabilities for fully automated malware analysis using sandbox technologies.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Recommended. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Suspicious payloads are detonated in a sandbox; behavioural reports are attached to the originating case and used for indicator extraction.

### Req. 38 — Capacity planning program

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to develop and implement a capacity planning program including: maintenance, monitoring, and evaluating of current SOC infrastructure, personnel, and overall service provider capacity and utilization with a maximum cap on resource utilization, to effectively manage service demand and ensure scalability of services.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Capacity planning is documented in `docs/operations/capacity-plan.md` with quarterly review. Utilization caps are enforced (e.g. ingestion EPS, analyst case-load).

### Req. 39 — Haseen portal requirements

**Requirement.** The Managed SOC Service Provider to implement requirements specified by NCA for Haseen portal.

**Tiers.** Tier 1: Required. Tier 2: Required.

**Solution.** Haseen integration in `src/integrations/haseen/` is the single entry/exit point for NCA-bound communications. Updates to the Haseen schema are tracked in that module's `CHANGELOG.md`.

## 7. Annex B — Event-log attribute requirements (TRMSOC Appendix B)

Every event ingested into the platform must carry the following attributes (where the source can supply them):

### 7.1 Logging attributes
- **Type** — type of event that occurred and its classification.
- **Place** — source of the event (source system, originating component).
- **Party concerned** — the system, account, or process the event relates to.
- **Time** — when the event occurred (ISO 8601 with timezone offset).
- **Result** — success or failure.

### 7.2 Event-log data fields
The platform's canonical event schema preserves at minimum:

- Unique event ID
- Accurate, formatted timestamp
- Event type / status code
- Device ID
- Session / transaction ID
- Autonomous System number
- Source IP (v4 and v6)
- Source port
- Destination IP (v4 and v6)
- Destination port
- Application identifier
- Status code
- Response time
- Additional fields (extensible map)
- Username and user ID
- Executed command
- Packet / data size

This schema is enforced at parse time; missing required attributes raise a schema-violation event for tuning.

## 8. Annex C — Minimum cybersecurity telemetry collection (TRMSOC Appendix C)

The retention model is **12 Months Active Storage + 18 Months Cold Data Storage** (i.e. logs must be retained for at least 18 months, with the most recent 12 months actively searchable). Packet capture is **72 hours**. The categories below must be collected.

### 8.1 Identity Management
- Account creation; credential type; establish/manage attributes (organization, groups/roles); manage and track changes in attributes and credentials; track usage of credentials; account deletion.
- Privileged: provisioning; deprovisioning; establish and manage privileges (privilege credentials); isolate, monitor, record, audit privilege sessions; control privileged actions (commands, tasks); track privilege escalation and delegation; monitor, alert, and respond to anomalous behaviours.

### 8.2 Email Filtering, Spam, and Phishing
- Email header, source IP, source domain.

### 8.3 Network Device Infrastructure (DHCP, DNS, NAT, Wi-Fi, IDS/IPS/NDR, Routing, Load Balancers, Web Proxy/WAF, VPN, Firewall, Database, Web Apps)
- DHCP lease information including MAC and IP.
- DNS — content of query/response/errors (all record types); zone transfer audit and content; passive DNS log (Tuple, Time_First, Time_Last, Count, Origin, source/overall query counts, response counts, query/response sizes).
- Wi-Fi supporting infrastructure logs (security at info level); device authentication logs (with user agent); URL browsing logs with HTTP methods; user authentication logs.
- Static NAT mapping and port forwards (date/time, protocol, port, IP mapping).
- IDS/IPS/NDR/SIEM logs; API activity logs; authentication logs; firewall logs; web proxy/WAF logs; network flow logs; remote access/VPN logs; DLP logs; DNS query/response logs.
- Routing tables, routing changes (CLI logging, BGP), IP addressing schema, authentication logs.
- Load balancer: connection type; date/time; resource ID; client/target IP:port; X-Forwarded-For; processing times; status codes; bytes sent/received; user agent; SSL protocol; matched rule priority; actions; redirect URL; error reason; target lists.
- Web proxy/WAF: source/destination hostname/IP/port/MAC; URL methods/user agent/decoded headers; URL categories; URL permitted/restricted/denied.
- Firewall: action (permit, teardown, close); interface; source/destination; protocol type; rule name and number; URL/user/user-agent/date.
- IDS/IPS/Wireless NDR: signature; anomaly; rate threshold; device name; event type/category; attack context (e.g. Fortinet); user agent; Wi-Fi channel; ESSID.
- VPN gateway (all events) — at minimum: accepts/teardowns/closes; date/time; source/destination; tunnel inside source/destination; auth info (user, device, agent); status changes; certificate validation.
- Database: addition of users (especially privileged); query and response/traceback (method, comments, embedded queries, alerts/failures, execution time); privilege elevation; structure changes; user role changes; DBA actions; logons (success/failure); failed logons; executable commands; CLI commands.
- Web applications and middleware: configuration version; database queries and response codes; user authentication (success/failure); user/admin app use (file/object access, audit log access, system access); application transactions (web hits, email send/receive, file transfers); transaction logs; system performance; service status changes; configuration and version.

### 8.4 Operating System (Windows, Linux, BIOS/UEFI, Cloud)
- OS audit records: date/time; event/status/error codes; service/command/application name; user or system account; device used; system start-up/shutdown; service start-up/shutdown; network connection changes; security setting changes.
- Logon attempts (success/failure); functions performed after logon; account changes; privileged account use; command-line actions; process execution.
- Application authentication; account changes; use of application privileges; application start-up/shutdown; failures; major configuration changes; transactions.
- File and object access; audit log access; system access/log-off; privilege access; RDP/SMB access; storage media install/removal.
- System performance: resource utilization; events; service status changes; service failures and restarts; process creation/termination.
- System configuration: changes to security configuration; audit-log clear; account changes; user/group management; scheduled task changes.
- Host network communications: listening ports/IPs; active comms; PowerShell execution and script-block logging; WMI events; CLI access.
- Linux specifics: SELinux/AppArmor warnings and violations; `/var/log/{messages,dmesg,syslog,daemon.log,cron,kern.log,boot.log,auth.log,secure,faillog,btmp,wtmp,utmp,mail.log,Xorg.X.log,dpkg.log,yum.log}`.
- BIOS/UEFI: version; created date; installed date; manufacturer.
- Cloud (general events): activity on break-glass accounts; conditional-access policy changes; environment policy changes (management logs); privileged role changes; VNet changes; deletion of delete-locks; logging policy changes; PIM and identity protection changes; alert-rule changes (audit the auditor); key-vault / KMS changes; storage-file access logs and hashes; baseline deviations for production app tiers.
- Cloud (general logging): IDS/IPS/NTA/NDR/SIEM logs; API activity logs; authentication logs; firewall logs; web proxy/WAF logs; service metrics; billing data; flow logs; remote access/VPN logs; system/OS logs; DLP logs; DNS query/response logs.
- Cloud (system config and performance): management actions (success/failure); admin login (success/failure).

### 8.5 Endpoint Detection & Response and Endpoint Security Tools
- Date and time; source hostname/IP/port; destination hostname/IP/port; description of malicious code or action and severity; identity or hash of file(s); description of action taken (clean, quarantine, delete); signature updates; indication of host that connected to a specific URL; date/time/IP and domain reputation; URL categorization.

### 8.6 Authentication and Authorization
- Authentication logons (success/failure); logoffs; privilege elevation (success/failure); security-related system alerts and failures.
- User and group additions, deletions, permission modifications.
- Unauthorized access attempts to critical systems and files.
- `sudo` / `runas`; enabling CLI access; system administrative commands; PowerShell execution and script-block logging.

### 8.7 Packet capture
- 72-hour rolling packet capture (full content where lawful).
- Decrypted plaintext where SSL offload is provided by the entity.
- Cleartext network traffic.
- Web application captures: URL, headers, HTTP methods (request body), HTTP response (response body), database queries, response codes.

The platform's onboarding workflow refuses to enable production monitoring for a beneficiary until each applicable category above is mapped to a working ingestion connector.

## 9. Traceability and audit

Every TRMSOC requirement above maps to:

1. A test in `tests/trmsoc/` that asserts the engineering control is in place.
2. A control statement in `docs/compliance/trmsoc-control-matrix.md` that names the owning team and the evidence artefact.
3. An entry in the `trmsoc-version` field of the platform's release manifest, allowing a release to be tagged as compliant with TRMSOC-1:2024.

Future revisions of TRMSOC will be tracked by republishing this document with a revised front-matter version and recording the diff in `docs/compliance/regulatory-revisions.md`.
